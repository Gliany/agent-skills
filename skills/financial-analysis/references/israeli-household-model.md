# Israeli household model

Use this reference for household analysis in Israel. Apply only definitions supported by the available data.

## Income

Keep these groups separate:

- `הכנסה קבועה`: net salaries, stable self-employment draws supported by history, recurring allowances, and other repeatable receipts.
- `הכנסה לא שוטפת`: reserve-duty compensation, annual bonuses, gifts, grants, tax refunds, one-time benefits, and investment income that is not a dependable monthly source.
- `החזר הוצאה`: insurance reimbursement, merchant refund, employer reimbursement, or reversal of a prior expense.
- `תקבול שאינו הכנסה`: internal transfer, family transfer whose nature is unclear, loan proceeds, sale of an asset, or another receipt that does not improve recurring earning power.

Use both average and median regular income when income varies. A savings plan should normally rely on regular income or on a clearly stated conservative baseline.

## Expenses and commitments

Classify each outflow along two dimensions:

1. Economic category: housing, arnona and municipal charges, utilities, food, transport and vehicle, insurance, health, children and childcare, pets, communications, subscriptions, restaurants, leisure, clothing, travel, taxes and fees, debt, savings, transfers, or unclassified.
2. Planning behavior:
   - `קבועה`: contractually or operationally recurring with limited monthly variation.
   - `משתנה חיונית`: necessary but controllable or usage-driven.
   - `משתנה גמישה`: deferrable or discretionary in the current household context.

Do not infer essentiality solely from merchant name. Childcare, medical care, accessibility, work travel, reserve-duty expenses, or pet medical care may be essential even when the category is usually flexible.

Israeli household examples include rent or mortgage, arnona, vaad bayit, electricity, water, gas, internet and cellular, kupat holim and supplemental cover, daycare or gan, tzaharon, school payments, chugim, Rav-Kav, Pango, vehicle licensing, and compulsory or comprehensive insurance.

## Credit cards and installments

- Reconcile the sum of posted card transactions to the related bank settlements for the same billing period.
- Avoid adding both the card transactions and the bank card debit to household spending.
- Treat a refund as a reduction of its original category when the relationship is known.
- For installments, record the current monthly installment as cash outflow and the remaining installments as a future commitment. Do not treat the full purchase again as current-month cash spending.
- Flag same-day, same-merchant, same-amount rows as possible duplicates only. Confirm card number, authorization, installment sequence, and refund activity before concluding.

## Loans

Separate principal, interest, fees, and insurance. Track outstanding balance, rate type, annual rate, remaining term, monthly payment, early-repayment cost, and collateral when sources provide them. Principal reduces debt but still consumes monthly cash. Compare early repayment with emergency-liquidity needs before recommending it.

## Savings and emergency fund

Separate liquid savings from pension, keren hishtalmut, education savings, securities, deposits with restrictions, and money earmarked for another purpose. Do not count mandatory payroll pension contributions as available household cash unless the analysis explicitly covers gross compensation and locked retirement assets.

Calculate emergency-fund months as:

`liquid emergency savings / verified essential monthly expenses`

Show scenarios such as three and six months rather than asserting one universal target. A household with children, variable income, a single primary earner, health exposure, or high fixed commitments may need the more conservative scenario.

## House-purchase target

Keep the target components visible:

- expected purchase price;
- required equity under the applicable financing assumption;
- legal, appraisal, brokerage, renovation, moving, and transaction costs when relevant;
- emergency cash that must remain after purchase;
- current dedicated capital;
- target date and monthly contribution;
- mortgage payment capacity based on sustainable cash flow.

Do not assume a mortgage percentage, tax treatment, eligibility, interest rate, or property cost without current evidence. Israeli mortgage and tax rules can change; verify current official sources when those rules affect the recommendation.

## Metrics

- `regular monthly surplus = regular monthly income - recurring operating expenses - required debt payments`
- `total cash-flow position = all verified cash receipts - all verified cash outflows`
- `savings rate = verified savings contribution / the explicitly stated income basis`
- `regular coverage ratio = regular income / cash commitments`
- `goal contribution gap = required goal contribution - sustainable monthly surplus`
- `total improvement required = max(0, recurring deficit) + required goal contribution`

State the period, inclusion rules, and whether transfers and principal are included for every headline measure.

## Opportunity analysis

Prioritize by verified annual impact and feasibility:

1. Stop confirmed errors, unused services, avoidable fees, and duplicate coverage.
2. Renegotiate recurring communications, insurance, banking, and service costs.
3. Set category limits for controllable food, restaurants, shopping, leisure, delivery, wallets, and subscriptions without assuming these are nonessential.
4. Restructure debt only after comparing total cost, rate, term, fees, and liquidity risk.
5. Identify income actions with a named source, realistic amount, lead time, costs, and probability. Do not book an opportunity as income before receipt.

For each recommendation show monthly impact, annual impact, evidence, difficulty, risk, owner, and next action.
